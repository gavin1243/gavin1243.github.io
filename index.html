About me

My Path Into Data, Software, and Healthcare

Background

I started out as a Mechanical Engineer in Mumbai. That background gave me a way of thinking about systems: how they're designed, where they fail, and what it takes to fix them. Moving to Boston for my MS in Data Analytics Engineering at Northeastern University was where that instinct met data science, and the work has been at that intersection ever since.

After graduating I moved across San Francisco, New York, and eventually Baltimore, mostly working in healthcare startups. I've spent most of that time in fast-paced environments where the work rarely fits neatly into one role.

I've had to wear multiple hats across data science, full-stack development, analytics, product thinking, and user support, often moving from a clinical problem to a dataset, then to an API, dashboard, or software feature that could help solve it.

A big part of my work has involved staying close to the people using the products. I've worked with nurses, physicians, directors of nursing, clinicians, and operations teams to understand where workflows break down, what data they actually need, and how software can make their day-to-day decisions easier. That feedback has shaped the way I build: start with the real problem, understand the user, then design a practical solution.

Over time, this has given me a broad view of how healthcare products are built and used in the real world. I've worked on machine learning models, patient monitoring dashboards, EMR integrations, clinical workflow tools, and full-stack applications. The common thread has always been the same: build useful systems that help teams act faster, make better decisions, and spend less time fighting messy processes.
